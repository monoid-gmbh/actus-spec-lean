\section{Overview}

This blueprint is a machine-checked re-authoring of the \emph{ACTUS Technical
Specification} --- the algorithmic standard for representing financial
contracts by their cash flows.  It reproduces the standard's normative content
(notations, utility functions, observers, and the per-contract schedule,
state-initialisation, state-transition and payoff tables) and links every
definition to a Lean~4 formalization, so that the document is at once the
specification and its proof of internal consistency.

It covers all eighteen contract types of the standard (\S\ref{sec:contracts}).
The stateful lending family (PAM, LAM, NAM, ANN, CLM) is modeled twice ---
relationally (the inductive \texttt{Step}, the readable spec) and functionally
(computable state-transition and payoff functions), with the two proven to
agree.  The remaining types --- positions, derivatives, swaps, credit
enhancement, and the exotic amortizer --- are direct cash-flow builders over the
shared schedule-generation engine.

The whole development is generic over the amount type and instantiated twice:
at \texttt{Float} for the executable engine --- validated against the ACTUS
reference test suite, where it reproduces all $236$ gated reference contracts
($3155$ cash flows) exactly --- and at the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ for the
quantitative metatheorems of \S\ref{sec:meta}.  The order/field structure those
bounds need exists over $\mathbb{R}$ but not over \texttt{Float} (where
$\mathtt{NaN}$ even breaks $a \le a$), so they are genuine theorems over
$\mathbb{R}$ rather than empirical checks.

\section{Notations}\label{sec:notations}

\subsection{Contract attributes}
Contract \emph{attributes} are the legal contractual terms that define a
contract's exchange of cash flows; they are catalogued in the ACTUS data
dictionary and referenced here by their dictionary short names in
\texttt{typewriter} font, e.g.\ $\attr{IED}$ for the \emph{initial exchange
date}.  Vector-valued attributes are indexed with a subscript: the $i$-th
element of $\attr{ARPRANX}$ is $\attr{ARPRANX}_i$.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\nul$}{null}-operator}
The symbol $\nul$ marks a quantity as \emph{undefined}: an optional attribute
that carries no value, or a schedule that contains no times
($\vec{t}^{IP}=\nul$ is the empty schedule).  In the Lean development this is the
\texttt{Option} type; an absent term is \texttt{none}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$t_0$}{t0}-time and state variables}
$t_0$ denotes the contract's status date $\attr{SD}$ --- the time as of which
its terms and implied state are represented; the contractual logic determines
events and states for all $t>t_0$.  \emph{State variables} describe the contract
state at a time $t$; they are written capitalised and bold and indexed by time,
e.g.\ $\svar{Nt}{t}$ is the outstanding notional principal at $t$.  The Lean
counterpart is a \texttt{State} record carrying \texttt{Nt}, \texttt{Ipnr},
\texttt{Ipac}, \texttt{Feac}, \texttt{Nsc}, \texttt{Isc}, \texttt{Sd}, etc.

\subsection{Contract events, STF and POF}
A contract \emph{event} $e_t^k$ is a scheduled or unscheduled occurrence at time
$t$ of type $k$, carrying an event time and a payoff $c\in\Real$, retrieved by
$\tev{\cdot}$ and $\fev{\cdot}$ respectively.  Two functions act on an event:
the \emph{state-transition function} $\stf{k}{CT}$ maps the pre-event state to
the post-event state, and the \emph{payoff function} $\pof{k}{CT}$ yields the
cash flow $\fev{e_t^k}$.  Both are specific to an event type $k$ and a contract
type $CT$.  In Lean these are the per-contract \texttt{stf} and \texttt{pof}
functions; the relational \texttt{Step} is the graph of \texttt{stf}.

\paragraph{Plain/primed state in a transition.}
The per-contract tables of Section~\ref{sec:contracts} use a light convention in
place of the standard's $t^-/t^+$ time decorations: a \emph{plain} state symbol
denotes its pre-event value (written $\sv{\cdot}$, the standard's
$\svar{\cdot}{t^-}$) and a \emph{primed} symbol its post-event value
($\svn{\cdot}$, the standard's $\svar{\cdot}{t^+}$).  An assignment such as
$\svn{Nt}=\sv{Nt}-\dots$ then reads ``new notional $=$ old notional $-\dots$'';
a primed symbol may also appear on the right-hand side when an update depends on
a value already revised earlier in the same event.  For the interest-bearing
contracts we further abbreviate the interest accrued on the outstanding notional
since the last state date by
\[ \dipac \;:=\; \yfr{\sv{Sd}}{t}\cdot\sv{Ipnr}\cdot\sv{Nt}, \]
the year fraction from the last state date $\sv{Sd}$ to the event time $t$ times
the nominal rate $\sv{Ipnr}$ times the notional $\sv{Nt}$; this term recurs in
almost every transition below.

\subsection{Event sequence and contract lifetime}
Events occurring at the same time are ordered by a fixed event-sequence
indicator, so that their STFs and POFs compose deterministically.  A contract's
\emph{lifetime} runs from $\attr{SD}$ to
$\min(\attr{MD},\attr{AMD},PR^*,\attr{STD},\attr{TD},\tmax)$, where $PR^*$ is the
principal-redemption event that drives $\svar{Nt}{}$ to $0$; terms that do not
apply to a given contract are taken as $+\infty$, and $\tmax$ optionally bounds
the analysis horizon for contracts without a natural end (e.g.\ STK).

\section{Utility functions}\label{sec:utils}

The standard is built on a small layer of \emph{utility functions} shared by
every contract.  The development is generic over the amount type
$\alpha$ (an ordered field for the metatheorems, \texttt{Float} for the engine).

\begin{definition}[Schedule function $S$]\label{def:schedule}
        \lean{Actus.Util.Schedule.schedule}
        \leanok
        The schedule function maps an anchor $s$, a cycle $c$ and an end date
        $T$ to the cyclic sequence
        \[
          \sdl{s}{c}{T}=\vec{t}=\left\{\begin{array}{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}
            \{\} & \text{if } s=\nul\land T=\nul\\
            s & \text{else if } T=\nul\\
            (s,T) & \text{else if } c=\nul\\
            (s=t_1,\dots,t_n=T) & \text{else}
          \end{array}\right.
        \]
        with $t_i<t_{i+1}$.  Cycles have the form $NPS$ ($N$ an integer, $P\in
        \{D,W,M,Q,H,Y\}$ a period unit, $S\in\{+,-\}$ a stub rule).  An optional
        boolean $B$ in $\sdll{s}{c}{T}{B}$ controls whether the end date $T$
        belongs to the schedule, and the \attrl{EOMC}/\attrl{BDC} conventions
        (below) may shift the resulting times, so the sequence need not be
        equidistant.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Array schedule $\vec{S}$]\label{def:arrayschedule}
        \lean{Actus.Util.Schedule.arraySchedule}
        \uses{def:schedule}
        \leanok
        Array schedules generalise $S$ to vector inputs
        $\vec{s}=(s_0,\dots,s_m)$, $\vec{c}=(c_0,\dots,c_m)$:
        \[
          \vsdl{\vec{s}}{\vec{c}}{T} =
            \big(\sdl{s_0}{c_0}{s_1-c_0},\dots,\sdl{s_m}{c_m}{T}\big),
        \]
        coinciding with $S$ for $m=1$.  Used by LAX (\S\ref{sec:lax}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[End-of-month and business-day conventions]\label{def:conventions}
        \lean{Actus.Util.Conventions.applyEOM,
              Actus.Util.Conventions.applyBDC,
              Actus.Util.Conventions.isBusinessDay}
        \uses{def:schedule}
        \leanok
        The end-of-month convention \attrl{EOMC} fixes whether cyclic times
        anchored on a short month-end fall on the same day or the month-end; the
        business-day convention \attrl{BDC} shifts a time off a non-business day
        (following/preceding, modified or not, calculate-before or -after the
        shift); and the business-day calendar $B:t\mapsto\{\text{true,false}\}$,
        selected by \attrl{CLDR}, decides which days are business days
        (\texttt{NoHoliday}, \texttt{MondayToFriday}, \dots).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Year-fraction convention $Y$]\label{def:yearFraction}
        \lean{Actus.Util.DayCount.yearFraction}
        \leanok
        The year-fraction interface $Y:s,t,\attr{DCC}\mapsto\Real$ gives the
        fraction of a year between two times under a day-count convention
        \attr{DCC} (Actual/360, Actual/365, 30E/360, Actual/Actual ISDA,
        Business/252).  Per-annum rates are accrued by multiplying with $Y$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Contract-role sign $R$]\label{def:sign}
        \lean{Actus.Util.Conventions.sign}
        \leanok
        The contract-role sign $R:\attr{CNTRL}\to\{-1,+1\}$ maps a contract role
        to $+1$ for a claim (asset) or $-1$ for an obligation, fixing the
        direction of every role-sensitive cash flow and state (a positive flow
        runs from \attrl{CPID} to \attrl{CRID}).  E.g.\ $\attr{RPA}\mapsto+1$,
        $\attr{RPL}\mapsto-1$, $\attr{GUA}\mapsto-1$, $\attr{OBL}\mapsto+1$.  We
        abbreviate $\sgn$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Annuity amount $A$]\label{def:annuity}
        \lean{Actus.Util.Schedule.annuity}
        \uses{def:yearFraction}
        \leanok
        The annuity amount sizes the constant ANN instalment so the notional is
        fully repaid by maturity:
        \[
          \ann{s}{T}{n}{a}{r}=(n+a)\,
            \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}1+r\,\yfr{t_i}{t_{i+1}}}
                 {1+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\prod_{j=i}^{m-1}1+r\,\yfr{t_j}{t_{j+1}}},
        \]
        with $a$ the accrued interest at $s$, $r$ the rate, and $t_1,\dots,t_m$
        the future PR-schedule times of the annuity (\S\ref{sec:ann}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Canonical payoff and settlement FX]\label{def:settlement}
        The canonical payoff of a contract aggregates all future event payoffs
        under frozen risk-factor conditions, $\payoff{x}{t}=\sum_{c\in C}c$.
        When a contract settles in a currency \attrl{CURS} other than its
        denomination \attrl{CUR}, payoffs are scaled by the settlement FX rate
        \[
          X_{\attr{CUR}}^{\attr{CURS}}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}
            1 & \text{if } \attr{CURS}=\nul \lor \attr{CURS}=\attr{CUR}\\
            \obs{rf}{\attr{CUR/CURS}}{t} & \text{else,}
          \end{array}\right.
        \]
        which appears as the leading factor of every payoff function below.
\end{definition}

\section{Composition and observers}\label{sec:composition}

\begin{definition}[Contract composition]\label{def:ctst}
        Combined contracts derive their payoff from \emph{child contracts}
        (underlyings) referenced through the attribute \attr{CTST}, a list of
        contract references each carrying an \texttt{object}, a \texttt{type} and
        a \texttt{role}.  We write $\attr{CTST}_{role}^{type}$ to query a
        referenced child (omitting the index of the first match), e.g.\
        $\attr{CTST}_{FirstLeg}^{Contract}$ for a swap's first leg or
        $\attr{CTST}_{Underlying}^{Contract}$ for an option's underlying.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Risk-factor observer $O$]\label{def:rfobs}
        The risk-factor observer is the interface through which a contract reads
        its uncertain environment.  In rate mode it returns a market state,
        $\obsfunc{rf}:i,t\mapsto\Real$, and in event mode it returns observed
        unscheduled events, $\obsfunc{ev}:i,k,t\mapsto\{e_t^k,\dots\}$ --- e.g.\
        $\obs{rf}{\attr{RRMO}}{t}$ for a reset reference rate, or
        $\obs{ev}{\attr{CID},\attr{PP}}{t}$ for observed prepayments.  The
        interface fixes structure only; implementations encode whatever
        assumption a forward-looking analysis requires.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Child-contract observer $U$]\label{def:cldobs}
        \uses{def:ctst}
        The child-contract observer lets a parent read its children: future
        events $\cldfunc{ev}:i,t,a\mapsto\{e_v^k,\dots\}$, a child state variable
        $\cldfunc{sv}:i,t,x,a\mapsto\Real$, or a child attribute
        $\cldfunc{ca}:i,x\mapsto y$ --- the basis for evaluating swaps, options
        and credit enhancement over their underlyings.
\end{definition}

\section{Contract types}\label{sec:contracts}

Each contract type is specified by three normative tables --- its
\emph{contract schedule} (which events occur and when), its \emph{state-variable
initialisation} (the state at $t_0$), and its \emph{state-transition and payoff
functions} (the effect of each event) --- reproduced below and linked to the
Lean \texttt{stf}/\texttt{pof}/\texttt{Step} definitions that formalize them.

\subsection{PAM: Principal at Maturity}\label{sec:pam}

PAM is the archetypal ACTUS contract and the building block of the whole
maturity family.  The entire principal (notional) is advanced at the initial
exchange date \attrl{IED} and returned in a single bullet repayment at maturity
\attrl{MD}; in between, interest accrues continuously and is paid out on the
interest-payment schedule \attr{IP}.  No principal is amortized during the term,
so the outstanding notional \svar{Nt}{} stays constant between \attrl{IED} and
\attrl{MD} --- which makes PAM the natural model for fixed-term bonds, bullet
loans and term deposits.

The contract state carries the outstanding notional \svar{Nt}{}, the nominal
rate \svar{Ipnr}{}, accrued interest \svar{Ipac}{} and fees \svar{Feac}{}, and
the notional/interest scaling indices \svar{Nsc}{}/\svar{Isc}{}.  Optional
features layer on through their own events: rate resets (\attr{RR}/\attr{RRF},
optionally clamped to the life window $[\attr{RRLF},\attr{RRLC}]$), index
scaling (\attr{SC}), periodic fees (\attr{FP}), prepayment penalties
(\attr{PY}), unscheduled prepayment (\attr{PP}) and early termination
(\attrl{TD}).  Because every other lending contract (LAM, NAM, ANN, CLM) is
specified as PAM plus a redemption rule, the PAM state-transition and payoff
functions below are reused throughout the family.

\subsection{LAM: Linear Amortizer}\label{sec:lam}

A linear amortizer repays principal in equal redemptions on the
principal-redemption schedule \attr{PR}, so the outstanding notional steps down
by a fixed amount each period and reaches zero exactly at maturity.  Interest is
charged on the declining balance, so the interest portion of each instalment
shrinks over time while the principal portion stays constant --- the classic
constant-amortization loan.

LAM, NAM and ANN share PAM's machinery and differ only in how the redemption is
sized: LAM fixes the principal portion (\attrl{PRNXT}); NAM fixes the total
payment and lets principal absorb whatever is left after interest; ANN fixes the
total payment through the annuity formula.  The LAM tables below therefore
record only what differs from PAM --- chiefly the \attr{PR} redemption and the
interest-calculation-base bookkeeping (\attrl{IPCB}) that tracks the balance on
which interest is charged.

\subsection{LAX: Exotic Linear Amortizer}\label{sec:lax}

The exotic amortizer generalizes LAM to non-uniform schedules.  Principal can
be both drawn down (\attr{PI}, principal increase) and redeemed (\attr{PR}) on
piecewise \emph{array} schedules (\S\ref{sec:utils}): the term is partitioned
into segments, each with its own anchor, cycle, rate and redemption amount.
This captures structured loans with grace periods, step-ups, balloon segments
and draw phases that a single cyclic schedule cannot express.

Within each segment the dynamics are exactly those of LAM; the array schedule
simply concatenates the per-segment cyclic schedules, so the state-transition
and payoff functions reduce to the PAM/LAM ones evaluated against the parameters
of the currently active segment.

\begin{definition}[LAXUMP]\label{def:laxump}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Execution.laxCashflows,
              Actus.Contract.Execution.umpCashflows}
        \uses{def:amortizers, def:annuity}
        \leanok
        LAX (exotic amortizer) redeems or draws principal on piecewise array schedules; UMP (\S\ref{sec:ump}) is a non-maturity deposit whose interest capitalizes, settling at the agreed price plus accrued interest on termination.
\end{definition}

\subsection{NAM: Negative Amortizer}\label{sec:nam}

In a negative amortizer the borrower pays a fixed total instalment
(\attrl{PRNXT}) each period, out of which interest is served first and only the
remainder reduces principal.  When the instalment exceeds the interest due the
loan amortizes normally; when interest exceeds the instalment the shortfall is
capitalized and the outstanding notional actually \emph{grows} --- hence
``negative'' amortization.

NAM is PAM with a \attr{PR} event whose redemption equals the fixed payment net
of accrued interest, with the interest-calculation base updated accordingly.  It
is the model for payment-capped and graduated-payment mortgages, where the
scheduled payment is held constant even when it fails to cover current
interest.

\subsection{ANN: Annuity}\label{sec:ann}

An annuity pays a constant total instalment over its life --- the annuity
amount $\annfunc$ of \S\ref{sec:utils} --- sized so that the level payments
exactly retire the loan by maturity.  Early instalments are mostly interest and
later ones mostly principal, but the total is fixed, which is what makes
annuities the standard structure for consumer credit and mortgage lending.

Because the instalment depends on the prevailing rate, a rate reset (\attr{RR})
triggers a re-amortization: the annuity amount is recomputed from the current
balance, the new rate and the remaining redemption schedule so the contract
still amortizes to zero by \attrl{MD}.  Apart from this re-sizing, ANN reuses
PAM's rate, interest and fee logic --- which is why the rate-bound metatheorem
(\ref{thm:rate-bound}) carries over to ANN for free.

\subsection{CLM: Call Money}\label{sec:clm}

Call money is a short-term, open loan that either party may ``call''
(terminate) at will, subject to a notice period.  It has no contractual
maturity: interest is not paid out but capitalizes into the notional through

maturity and the capitalization of interest, CLM follows PAM, so it reuses PAM's
state transitions directly.

\subsection{FXOUT: Foreign Exchange Outright}\label{sec:fxout}

An FX outright is an agreement to exchange two fixed notionals in different
currencies on a single future settlement date at a pre-agreed rate.  One leg
delivers \attrl{NT} units of the base currency and the other delivers the
counter-currency amount; settlement may be physical (both legs exchanged) or in
net cash.

The payoff is therefore the signed difference of the two delivery legs,
converted at the settlement-currency rate.  The conservation metatheorem
(\ref{thm:conservation-deriv}) records that the two legs net exactly to the par
difference $R\cdot(\attr{NT}_1-\attr{NT}_2)$ at unit FX.  FXOUT is one of the
cash-settled derivatives formalized as \ref{def:derivative}.

\subsection{SWPPV: Plain Vanilla Interest Rate Swap}\label{sec:swppv}

A plain-vanilla interest-rate swap exchanges a fixed-rate and a
floating-rate interest stream on a common notional over a sequence of periods.
On each payment date the fixed leg pays $N\cdot\attr{fix}\cdot Y$ and the
floating leg pays $N\cdot\attr{flt}\cdot Y$ for the period year-fraction $Y$;
the contract may settle the two legs gross (both flows) or net (their
difference).

which the formalization proves equals the difference of the two gross legs
(\ref{thm:conservation-deriv}).  SWPPV and the general SWAPS are formalized as
\ref{def:swap}.

\subsection{SWAPS: Swap}\label{sec:swaps}

The general swap is a \emph{composite} contract: it references two child
contracts --- its two legs --- runs each through the ordinary execution engine,
and then combines their cash flows.  The legs may be any ACTUS contracts (for
instance a fixed-rate and a floating-rate PAM or ANN), and the swap settles them
either gross (passing both legs' flows through) or net (offsetting same-date
flows).

This compositional definition is what lets ACTUS express swaptions, asset swaps
and other structured exchanges as hierarchies of simpler contracts
(\S\ref{sec:composition}).  SWAPS is formalized with SWPPV as \ref{def:swap}.

\subsection{CAPFL: Cap--Floor}\label{sec:capfl}

A cap (floor) is a rate option layered on a variable-rate underlying: it pays
the holder whenever the underlying's reset rate rises above the cap (falls below
the floor), compensating the rate excess on the underlying's notional.  It is
the standard hedge against adverse rate moves on a floating-rate loan.

CAPFL is defined over a child contract: at each of the underlying's reset dates
it observes the would-be reset rate $r$ and pays $\max(r-\attr{cap},0)$ or
$\max(\attr{floor}-r,0)$ on the period's notional and year-fraction.  The
intrinsic-value metatheorem (\ref{thm:intrinsic}) proves these payoffs are
always non-negative.  Formalized with the derivatives as \ref{def:derivative}.

\subsection{OPTNS: Option}\label{sec:optns}

\subsection{FUTUR: Future}\label{sec:futur}

A future is a linear forward on an underlying: at settlement it pays the
difference between the observed underlying price $S$ and the agreed contract
(futures) price $F$, that is $S-F$, scaled by the contract size.  Unlike an
option it is symmetric --- the holder gains or loses linearly with the
underlying --- so its payoff may take either sign.

FUTUR is the simplest of the derivative builders defined over a child
underlying, and is formalized with them as \ref{def:derivative}.

\subsection{CEG: Credit Enhancement Guarantee}\label{sec:ceg}

A credit-enhancement guarantee is a third-party promise to cover the losses
of one or more \emph{covered} contracts.  The guarantor receives a premium at
inception and, if a covered contract suffers an observed credit event, pays out
the covered exposure --- the sum of the covered contracts' outstanding
notionals plus, for a guarantee, their accrued interest.

CEG is a composed contract: it references its covered contracts through
\attr{CTST} (\S\ref{sec:composition}) and reads their state through the
child-contract observer to size the exposure at the moment of default.  The
payout therefore carries the guarantor's role sign and is bounded below by zero
(\ref{thm:credit-bound}).  CEG and the collateral variant CEC are formalized as
\ref{def:credit}.

\noindent\emph{Divergence from the reference data.}  Here the formalization is the
arbiter.  The executable engine reproduces every gated reference contract exactly,
but one CEG reference case (\texttt{guarantee14}) carries a reference value that
disagrees with the \attr{CEG} payoff specified below --- the engine computes the
value this specification prescribes.  We record it as a known discrepancy in the
reference suite rather than a defect of the specification.

\subsection{CEC: Credit Enhancement Collateral}\label{sec:cec}

Credit-enhancement collateral is a guarantee backed by posted assets rather
than a counterparty's promise: on a covered credit event the protection pays the
covered exposure, but \emph{capped} at the observed value of the collateral.
The payout is thus
$\min(\text{coverage}\cdot\text{exposure},\ \text{collateral value})$ --- it can
never exceed either the covered claim or the collateral on hand.

This cap is exactly the bound proved in \ref{thm:credit-bound}.  CEC is
formalized with CEG as \ref{def:credit}.

\section{Metatheorems}\label{sec:meta}

The formalization adds guarantees the original specification never states: the
relational and functional models agree, status dates advance monotonically,
maturity zeroes the notional, and --- over $\mathbb{R}$ --- a battery of
quantitative bounds and conservation laws hold of every execution.

\begin{theorem}[Relational/functional agreement]\label{thm:agree}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Agree.pam_step_to_fun}
        \uses{def:pam-step}
        \leanok
        Every relational PAM step lands on the functional next-state for some
        event and time; conversely every admissible event/time yields a step.
        The relation is exactly the graph of \texttt{stf}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Status-date monotonicity]\label{thm:mono}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.pam_trace_mono}
        \uses{thm:agree}
        \leanok
        Along any execution trace the status date is non-decreasing: each step
        requires $\mathtt{Sd} \le t$ and sets $\mathtt{Sd} := t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Maturity]\label{thm:maturity}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.pam_md_nt}
        \uses{def:pam-stf}
        \leanok
        The maturity event zeroes the notional principal (and accrued interest
        and fees); termination additionally zeroes the interest rate.
\end{theorem}

        After a rate reset the nominal rate lies within the life floor/cap
        window $[\mathtt{RRLF}, \mathtt{RRLC}]$ (whenever both are present and
        well-ordered).  Holds for PAM and, by reuse of PAM's rate logic, for
        ANN.  With every cap/floor absent the reset instead returns the raw
        repricing target $O^{rf}(\mathtt{RRMO},t)\cdot\mathtt{RRMLT}+\mathtt{RRSP}$
        (\texttt{pam\_rr\_uncapped}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Redemption non-overshoot]\label{thm:no-overshoot}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.redeemed_no_overshoot,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.lam_pr_nt_mem,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.redeemed_abs_le}
        \uses{def:amortizers}
        \leanok
        A principal redemption never overshoots: with a sign-normalised
        nonnegative state the post-redemption notional stays in $[0, \mathtt{Nt}]$,
        and the redeemed amount never exceeds the outstanding notional in
        magnitude ($|\,\mathrm{redeemed}\,| \le |\mathtt{Nt}|$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Strict amortization and full repayment]\label{thm:amortization}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.lam_pr_nt_lt,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.lam_pr_full_repay}
        \uses{thm:no-overshoot}
        \leanok
        With a strictly positive notional and instalment a redemption strictly
        decreases the notional (guaranteed progress); once the instalment
        reaches the outstanding notional the step drives it to exactly $0$
        (clean repayment).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Interest capitalization conserves value]\label{thm:ipci}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.pam_ipci_capitalizes}
        \uses{def:pam-stf}
        \leanok
        The \texttt{IPCI} event moves accrued interest into the notional and
        resets accrued interest to $0$: value is relocated, not created.  (This
        one holds definitionally at any amount type.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[Principal-redemption trace]\label{def:prtrace}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.PRTrace}
        \uses{def:amortizers}
        \leanok
        \texttt{PRTrace} is the sub-relation of the LAM execution trace
        consisting of zero or more admissible principal-redemption (\texttt{PR})
        steps --- the regime in which the notional should be conserved (events
        like \texttt{IED}/\texttt{IPCI} reset or grow it).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Trace-level conservation of the notional]\label{thm:conservation}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.prTrace_nt_mem,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.prTrace_abs_le,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.prTrace_isTrace}
        \uses{def:prtrace, thm:no-overshoot}
        \leanok
        Along an entire principal-redemption trace the notional stays in
        $[0, \mathtt{Nt}_0]$ and never increases in magnitude --- the
        single-step non-overshoot lifted to whole executions by induction over
        the trace.  Every \texttt{PRTrace} is moreover a genuine LAM execution
        trace, so this is a statement about real runs.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Payoff bounds for the derivative and credit families (over $\mathbb{R}$)}

\begin{theorem}[Cap/floor and option intrinsics are non-negative]\label{thm:intrinsic}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.capfl_intrinsic_nonneg,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.option_call_intrinsic_nonneg,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.option_put_intrinsic_nonneg,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.option_call_pos_iff}
        \uses{def:derivative}
        \leanok
        The cap/floor rate-excess kernel
        $\max(r - \mathtt{cap}, 0) + \max(\mathtt{floor} - r, 0)$ and the
        European call/put intrinsics $\max(S - K, 0)$, $\max(K - S, 0)$ are all
        $\ge 0$, so a long cap, floor, or option never settles negative; a call
        is in the money exactly when the underlying exceeds the strike.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Settlement conservation for FX and swaps]\label{thm:conservation-deriv}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.fxout_delivery_sum,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.swppv_net_eq_legs}
        \uses{def:swap, def:derivative}
        \leanok
        An FX outright's two delivery legs net to the par difference
        $R \cdot (\mathtt{Nt_1} - \mathtt{Nt_2})$ at unit FX; a swap's per-period
        fixed and floating legs net to $R \cdot N \cdot (\mathtt{fix} -
        \mathtt{flt}) \cdot Y$ --- exactly what the net-settlement fold computes
        from the two gross legs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Credit-enhancement payout bounds]\label{thm:credit-bound}
        \lean{Actus.Contract.Properties.cec_payout_le_value,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.cec_payout_le_claim,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.ceg_exposure_nonneg,
              Actus.Contract.Properties.foldl_add_nonneg}
        \uses{def:credit}
        \leanok
        A collateral payout $\min(\mathtt{coverage}\cdot\mathtt{exposure},
        \mathtt{value})$ never exceeds either the posted collateral value or the
        covered claim; the covered exposure, a sum of per-leg magnitudes, is
        non-negative, so the payout carries the contract-role sign.
\end{theorem}
